# Hostinger: Decode the Noise

Signal Fire watches the documented Pravda publishing network across the Baltics and Finland. It finds copied stories, traces their cited origins, and searches for matching claims in public conversation. A fixed rule measures wording, shared links, timing and available account evidence. The Hostinger AI Router checks narrative agreement and weighs claims against selected trackers, with an independent evidence-relation check.

Every finding carries reasons, source passages, public links and an exportable pack. Missing account histories stay unknown. In a frozen 64-set replay, it caught 7 of 32 network sets and falsely flagged 0 of 32 controls. Twenty-five misses exposed sparse retrieval; the small sample cannot establish a general false-flag rate. The service runs on a Hostinger VPS. It flags coordination whoever is behind it, and never reports or removes posts.

# Context.dev: The Web Is Your Real-Time Database

The Web, Watched. Signal Fire turns changes on public war-news pages and channels into inspectable evidence. Context.dev monitors notify the VPS through signed webhooks. The receiver verifies and deduplicates each event, then queues collection, grouping, measured signals and claim checking in a separate worker.

Parsers read public social platforms; scraping traces article copies and Telegram origins. Search retrieves reporting and earlier recorded cases. Structured extraction checks source fields, batch collects indexes, and URL mapping reaches a fourteen-day publication window. The app exposes actual requests and measured step times. Method shows endpoint latency, credits and first-post delays, including slow historical collection. Matching public conversation is distinguished from a proven copying connection. Anyone can create a private watch for another conflict or known network. The monitor continues with the Mac off.
