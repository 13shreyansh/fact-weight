How Signal Fire works

It starts from no list of suspects. It reads a country’s news, finds who copies it, and lets the copying pattern decide who belongs together.

1. A country’s stories

For each country we collect about 40 of the week’s most covered stories about or for that country, using Context.dev news search and Exa, in the country’s main languages and English. Each story keeps its headline, outlet, link, language and publication time.

2. Finding copies

From each story we take two or three distinctive sentences and search the open web for near-identical text. Every candidate page is fetched and compared word by word. A page counts as a copy when at least 60% of its word sequences match (shingle overlap of 0.6 or more). We record the site, link, publication time, language and any source it cites.

3. Groups

Two sites are linked each time they copy the same story within 60 minutes of each other. A group needs at least 3 sites and 3 shared stories. The linking threshold was tuned on one set of countries and frozen before we looked at the others.

4. Who they are

For each group: how old the domains are (from public registration records), shared hosting or network, shared page templates or analytics codes, which sources they cite most, and which languages and countries they target. If a group matches a network documented in published research (for example CheckFirst’s open data, DFRLab or VIGINUM), we name the research and link it. Otherwise the group says “Not named in published research”. Nothing in the search or the scoring uses a list of known sites.

5. What they want

We group the headlines each network pushes about the country into lines of argument, and compare each copy with the closest mainstream article to show the phrases the copy added. A label is only shown when two independent AI model families on the Hostinger AI Router agree on it; label words come from the DISARM and EUvsDisinfo vocabularies.

6. The organic control

Many stories are covered by dozens of outlets. When each outlet uses its own wording and its own timing, the story is marked Looks organic, with the reasons. This keeps the tool honest about what normal news looks like.

Verdicts

Looks coordinated: strongA group copied many of the country’s stories, fast and near-verbatim.
Looks coordinated: moderateThe pattern is there, but with fewer stories, slower copies or fewer sites.
Looks organicBroad coverage with independent wording and timing.
Not enough dataToo few copies or timestamps to tell.

Signal Fire never labels anything fake, propaganda or bots. It describes copying behaviour that anyone can check by opening the links.

What it cannot see

  • X (Twitter) is not covered. We do not buy or scrape X data.
  • Pages that search engines have not indexed, closed groups and private messaging.
  • Copies that are rewritten heavily enough to fall below the 60% match.
  • Who pays for a network. Shared infrastructure suggests common operation; it does not prove ownership.

Freshness and cost

30 countries are read again every 6 hours on our server. Any other country can be read on request; daily caps on search and AI calls keep the service affordable, and when they are reached the site says when builds resume.
