Fact Weight helps a reader compare a specific claim with specific documents. It does not decide whether a person is a bot, establish an author's intent, or treat an outlet as an absolute authority.

How a label is made

  1. You paste a post and choose up to four article URLs, or ask Context.dev to search a curated reference pool.
  2. Context.dev extracts their text. Fresh mode requests a new extraction. Retrieval time and any provider cache metadata stay in the evidence record.
  3. Hostinger AI Router extracts checkable claims from the post alone. A second call compares only those claim IDs with the retrieved passages. Extra claim IDs from the comparison cannot enter the result.
  4. Code normalizes Unicode and whitespace, then rejects quotations that do not occur in the extracted text. It groups articles from the same domain and near-identical extracted text.
  5. A different model family checks proposed relations. It can veto a relation but cannot create evidence. This check is not calibrated against human labels.
  6. Accepted supporting and contradicting groups determine the label.
Retrieved passages Label
Support, no contradiction Supported
Support and contradiction Contested
Contradiction, no support Contradicted
No direct support or contradiction Unsupported

One source group is enough for a relation label. It is not enough to establish a fact. Group counts describe documents, not votes, and are never converted into a probability.

Where this can fail

A quotation match only proves that those words were in the extraction. The model can still misunderstand them. Separate domains may copy a single wire report, share ownership, or repeat the same official statement. The grouping heuristic cannot prove independence.

You choose the documents, or discovery searches ERR, LRT, LSM, the EU Council, NATO and ISW. This pool is incomplete and institutionally skewed. An incomplete or biased source selection gives an incomplete view. A page can be stale, blocked, mistranslated, wrong or missing context. Retrieval time is not publication time. This version does not run a continuous feed.

Our evaluation includes synthetic cases and agent-constructed assertions against real documents. It does not establish real-world disinformation precision, human agreement, multilingual accuracy or calibrated confidence. Those require an independently labelled source corpus and review.

Privacy and limits

Your post and extracted passages go to Hostinger AI Router. Article URLs and, when discovery is enabled, up to 400 post characters go to Context.dev. The application does not write your post to disk. It holds up to 200 extracted pages in process memory for five minutes of reuse, and remembers the previous extracted text hash until cache eviction or restart. For comparison, at most the first 18,000 normalized characters per page are sent to the model. Important later passages can be missed. Page hashes cover the entire extracted text. Provider data handling applies. Avoid private or personal information.

The demo limits each connection to four requests per minute, two concurrent analyses, four sources per request and 150 analyses per process per day. The counter resets on a process restart, so it is a demo abuse limit rather than a durable billing cap.
