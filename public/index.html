Copied story

A sorting office for the open web, running now

Real outlets write their own words on their own schedule. Coordinated sites publish the same text within minutes, story after story. Signal Fire watches for that rhythm around the clock, in every language, and shows its working.

  1. Collect

    Each country’s most covered stories of the week, read in its own languages and English.

    • Exa neural search
    • Context.dev news search
    • Date-filtered, per language

  2. Match

    Distinctive sentences are hunted across the web; every candidate page is fetched and compared.

    • Exact-phrase web search
    • Context.dev scrape
    • Shingle overlap ≥ 0.6
    • Embedding similarity check

  3. Graph

    Sites become linked each time they copy the same story within an hour. Communities in that graph are the groups.

    • Co-copy graph, 60 min window
    • Louvain communities
    • Threshold frozen on held-out countries

  4. Attribute

    Who runs it: the infrastructure and sources a group shares, matched to published research only at the end.

    • RDAP domain age
    • IP and network (ASN)
    • Analytics and tag-manager IDs
    • Page-template fingerprint
    • Telegram origin tracing

  5. Interpret

    What they want: the lines they push, and the words a copy adds that mainstream coverage never had.

    • Spin diff vs closest mainstream
    • Two AI model families must agree
    • DISARM and EUvsDisinfo vocabulary

  6. Watch

    Every group’s pages stay under watch, so new copies land here without a rebuild, and so do your alerts.

    • Context.dev monitors + webhooks
    • Re-read every 6 hours
    • TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Reddit pickup
    • Alerts API for newsrooms

    Three verdicts, no accusations

    Signal Fire describes behaviour it can measure. It never calls anything fake, propaganda or bots.

    Looks coordinated

    Three or more sites copied the same stories within an hour, repeatedly. Marked strong or moderate by how often and how fast.

    Looks organic

    Many outlets covered it, each with its own wording and timing. The control: what normal news looks like.

    Not enough data

    Too few copies or timestamps to say either way. We say so instead of guessing.
